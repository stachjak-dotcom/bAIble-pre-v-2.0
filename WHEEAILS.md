# 🛞 WheeAIls

## Small wheels for a big bAIble

bAIble can become large because real collaboration becomes large.

That does **not** mean a new person has to learn all of it first.

**WheeAIls are the small helping wheels for getting started.**

They are not another architecture layer, software package, or test you have to pass.
They are simply a simpler path through bAIble when the whole thing feels like too much.

## The shortest path

1. **Tell the AI what you want to accomplish.**
2. **Let the AI help separate what is known, assumed, and unknown.**
3. **Choose one small, reversible first step.**
4. **Do the step.**
5. **Check whether the result is actually what you wanted.**
6. **Keep what matters and continue.**

If something important is unclear, stop and ask rather than letting the AI invent the missing rule.

## You can use the words too

You do not need the whole bAIble vocabulary, but these six words are useful when you want to be precise:

- **Explore** — investigate without changing the project.
- **Propose** — suggest a solution without implementing it.
- **Prepare** — get a change ready without applying it.
- **Implement** — make the requested change within scope.
- **Verify** — check whether the result actually works.
- **Review** — look for defects, risks, regressions, or scope problems.

For a deeper explanation, see [FOUNDATION_V0_1.md](FOUNDATION_V0_1.md).

## When to take the wheels off

WheeAIls have done their job when you no longer need the simplified path.

You can then use bAIble more directly, introduce the parts that solve a real problem, and ignore the rest until you need them.

> **The wheels are not the destination. They are there so you can start moving.**

## One rule underneath all of this

**AI can help you act, but fluent output is not proof.**

When something matters, check it against reality.
